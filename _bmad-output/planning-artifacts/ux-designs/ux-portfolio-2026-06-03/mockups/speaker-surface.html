Signature talks
Senior · Staff+All levels welcome

Patterns That Survive Hype Cycles: from CORBA to Kubernetes Assumption

Three decades of "this changes everything" — and the handful of ideas that actually did. A veteran IC's field guide to telling durable architecture from fashion.

Abstract

Every era ships a technology that promises to end complexity — distributed objects, SOA, microservices, serverless, now agents. Most of the promises evaporate; a few patterns quietly survive and compound. Having shipped through every one of those waves as a hands-on engineer, I'll walk through what actually persisted and why: the bet on explicit contracts over clever coupling, on boring boundaries over magic, on observability before scale. We'll trace a single thread — how a request travels — from CORBA's ORB through REST, message buses, and Kubernetes service meshes, and see how the same three or four forces reassert themselves each time under new names. Then we turn to agentic systems and ask the only useful question: which of today's "everything is different now" claims rhyme with patterns that lasted, and which rhyme with the ones that didn't. You'll leave able to interrogate the next hype cycle with a veteran's checklist rather than a vendor's slide.

You'll leave with
  • A checklist for separating durable architecture from fashion
  • The three forces that reassert themselves every cycle
  • How today's agentic claims map onto patterns that lasted (and didn't)
  • A vocabulary for pushing back on hype without being the "no" person

Shipping with a Team of Agents: the BMAD Method in Practice Assumption

How I run real features through a disciplined, auditable agent workflow — and how this very site was built that way, in the open.

Read the abstract ▾
Agentic coding demos look magical and ship nothing. This talk is the opposite: the unglamorous discipline that makes agents dependable teammates. I'll show the BMAD Method end to end — brief, brainstorm, research, spec, architecture, stories, review, retro — using a real project as the worked example: the portfolio site you may have arrived on, whose entire build history is published as it happens. We'll look at where agents genuinely accelerate a 30-year engineer, where they need a hard human gate, and how to keep every step auditable so the work is trustworthy rather than just fast. The throughline: treat the agent as an IC you mentor and review, not an oracle you obey.
You'll leave with
  • A repeatable, auditable agent workflow you can adopt Monday
  • Where to place the human gates that actually matter
  • How to keep agentic work trustworthy, not just fast

The Senior Engineer's Edge in the Age of Agents Assumption

Why thirty years of judgment is an advantage, not a liability, when the tools can suddenly write code. A reframe for experienced ICs wondering where they fit.

Read the abstract ▾
The anxious story says agents make seniority obsolete. The honest one is the reverse: agents amplify taste, and taste is what experience buys. This talk makes the case — with concrete before/after examples — that the scarce skill is no longer typing code but framing problems, reviewing output, and knowing which corners are load-bearing. I'll share how a veteran IC restructures their day around agents, what to delegate and what to never delegate, and how to mentor a team through the transition without either cynicism or hype.
You'll leave with
  • A reframe of seniority as leverage, not legacy
  • A concrete delegate / never-delegate split for agentic work
  • How to bring an experienced team along without hype or fear
